An atlas of where records come from

Every record
has ancestors.

Provenance charts them: 3,513 albums set on one unbroken timeline, joined by 15,557 documented connections — who covered whom, who sampled whom, who heard a record and made a different one because of it. It looks like a night sky. It reads like a bibliography.

LAW I

Time is sacred

A record's place on the x-axis is its release date — always, at every zoom, in every view. Nothing ever moves off the timeline to make the picture prettier. Influence can only flow from left to right, because that is the only direction it has ever flowed.

LAW II

No edge without evidence

Every line in the sky carries at least one receipt: a claim, paraphrased; a source, linked; a verification pass that read the source back and checked the claim against it. A connection that cannot cite its source does not exist here. 16,162 of 16,410 receipts are independently verified.

How a record enters the sky

  1. RESOLVEA title and an artist become a canonical identity — a MusicBrainz release group — through a calibrated matcher that survives reissues, editions, deluxe-remaster noise, and “The Beatles, The”.
  2. HARVESTStructured facts arrive first: personnel from liner data, same-artist lineage, documented artist influence — born verified, no model involved.
  3. READA language model reads the record's encyclopedia entry — in the record's own language first; Clube da Esquina is read in Portuguese — and extracts influence claims under a strict contract.
  4. VERIFYEvery claim is checked against its cited source by a second, independent pass. Claims that contradict their source never publish. Fabricated citations die here.
  5. CHARTSurviving connections join the Bibliotheca — the shared sky — where every line you see can be traced back, receipt by receipt, to the sentence it came from.

How to read it

Brightness is connection — the most-quoted records burn. Fog is density: thousands of records below the magnitude limit, resolving into named stars as you zoom, hover, or search. Amber is the only signal color, and it is spent on exactly one thing: evidence.
